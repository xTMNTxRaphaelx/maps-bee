# Trimming the 2x2 to the tiles the crop covers, 2026-10-16

The client fetches four tiles. The thumbnail is 640 x 360 and a tile renders at 640, so the crop is
exactly one tile wide — both columns always — and 360 of 640 tall, so it reaches into the second row
only when the pin sits within 180 px of the row boundary. The other 43.75% of the time, two of the
four tiles are fetched and never drawn.

Measured rate over a 4,160-point grid across the Jaipur z15 box (test/trim-cost.js): **44.6%**.

## What it saves in tile payload (scripts/address-shapes.js, shape D)

225 points over four cities, the same fixtures that settled the z15 question.

| city | B tiles/pt | D tiles/pt | B KiB/pt | D KiB/pt | points trimmed |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Jaipur  | 4.0 | 3.3 | 40.6  | 34.0  | 21/60 |
| Lugano  | 3.9 | 2.9 | 99.4  | 72.5  | 23/45 |
| Berlin  | 3.8 | 3.1 | 304.9 | 245.5 | 25/60 |
| Nairobi | 3.9 | 3.2 | 248.5 | 202.0 | 23/60 |

Totals: tiles 877.5 -> 706.5 (-19.5%), payload 40,113 -> 32,152 KiB (-20%), 92/225 points trimmed.

## What it costs the address: nothing measurable

Shape D scored identically to shape B on **every part in all four cities** — road, neighbourhood,
suburb, village, city, housenumber. Not one hit moved.

The reason is that the parts with the longest reach do not come from the tiles. SUBURB_M is 3,000 m
and a z15 tile is about 1.1 km at this latitude, so suburb was already a gazetteer part; what the
dropped row would have supplied is a second tile-row of roads and pois, and the nearest road or poi
to the pin is in the crop by construction.

## What it saves a real client: about a twentieth of that

results/address-shapes.txt's own correction says payload is not client cost. It is right again here.
Cold reads from a fresh client over a delayed link, full 2x2 against the trimmed one, cell plus
label plus areas (test/trim-cost.js, 7 points x 2 latencies, atlas core.length 1437):

```
point            crop   blocks        bytes
26.7985,75.9037  2/4    13 -> 12      174,023 -> 166,431
26.8503,75.8504  2/4    15 -> 14      245,987 -> 239,992
26.8984,75.8012  2/4    17 -> 14      288,812 -> 235,558
26.9465,75.7479  2/4    15 -> 14      237,466 -> 228,612
27.0057,75.6946  2/4    14 -> 13      200,810 -> 196,038
26.8355,75.6700  4/4    15 -> 15      unchanged, the crop covers all four
26.9613,75.6700  4/4    16 -> 16      unchanged
```

On the trimmed points: blocks 74 -> 67 (**0.91x**), bytes 1,147,098 -> 1,066,631 (**0.93x**).
Weighted by the 44.6% of points that trim at all: **about 4% of blocks and 3% of bytes** off a
random preview, against the 20% the payload table predicts. The two dropped tiles mostly live in
blocks the read fetches anyway for the two it keeps.

Identical at both latencies — the saving is blocks, not round trips.

## Decided 2026-10-16: not doing it

A real saving, for no measured accuracy cost, and still not worth it. Three reasons, in order:

1. **0.6 blocks.** 4% off a 13-17 block preview, against prefetchTop(2) taking Jaipur from 60 blocks
   to 13. The trim cannot do better than this: its ceiling is the blocks the dropped tiles do not
   already share with the two kept, and the table above says that is usually one.
2. **It would tie the fetch to the renderer.** preview.js would read W, H and TILE from
   render-svg.js, so changing the thumbnail's shape would silently change what every client
   downloads and how far deriveAddress can see — a rendering tweak with an accuracy consequence
   nobody would think to re-measure.
3. **An unmeasured failure mode at coverage boundaries.** readTiles returns null when every tile it
   asked for is missing. Trimmed, it asks for two, so where z15 is a filler run on the ocean side of
   a coastline the crop's two tiles can be absent while the dropped row's two exist, and the walk
   falls to z14 where it renders z15 today. That may well look better; nothing here measured it.

The code was removed rather than left unused: no `cropMask`, no shape D in address-shapes.js, no
test/trim-cost.js. This file is the record, so the next person does not re-run it to learn the same
thing. If client cost ever becomes the binding constraint, the accuracy answer above is already
banked and the geometry is three lines.
